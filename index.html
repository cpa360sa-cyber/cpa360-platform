The real problem — in the Department's own figures

Four in five CPAs are non-compliant.
It is an institutional problem, not a land problem.

Of 1,743 registered CPAs, the Department of Land Reform and Rural Development reports 207 fully compliant, 124 partially compliant and 1,385 non-compliant with the annual duties in Regulations 8 and 9 (CPA Annual Report 2024/25, briefed to Parliament on 15 October 2025). Non-compliance had already risen from 75% in 2022/23 to 82% in 2023/24. The Department's own explanation is institutional: disputes between members, untraceable members who make an AGM quorum impossible, and claimants who were never fully verified.

  • Weak governance systems

    1,385 CPAs did not meet their annual duties under the CPA Act in 2024/25 — holding an AGM, keeping minutes, and filing committee lists and financial statements.

  • Incomplete beneficiary records

    Untraceable members and unverified claimants are why many CPAs cannot reach an AGM quorum. Disputes and succession cases stay open.

  • Poor document control

    Every annual return needs financial statements, minutes, a membership list, a committee list and land-transaction reports — drawn from records that are scattered or lost.

  • Financial control gaps

    Financial statements are due within two months of the AGM. That is hard to meet without steady budgets, bookkeeping and reconciliations.

  • Unclear land allocation

    Claimants, farm dwellers and labour tenants often hold overlapping rights on the same land, and allocations and leases go undocumented or contested.

  • Underutilised assets

    Some CPAs were formed only for security of tenure, with no economic activity. Infrastructure and equipment are recorded nowhere and rarely maintained.

  • Weak project management

    Projects without business cases, owners or progress tracking — and no evidence trail for the partners who back them.

  • Limited commercial readiness

    Forestry, agri-processing and conservation partners need a CPA that can evidence its governance, land records and decision-making.

  • Funding readiness gaps

    Funders ask for a lawful committee, verified members and clean financial statements. Proposals stall when that evidence is missing.

  • Poor performance visibility

    In October 2025, MPs asked for the total land held and membership size across all 1,743 CPAs. The Department undertook to provide it in writing.

The CPA360 platform

One platform. One institutional view.

CPA360 turns what the CPA Act and the Registrar expect into everyday systems. Every institutional function sits in one operating environment — so the Committee, the administration office, the Department and funders all work from the same verified record.

  1. Governance

    EXCO, committees, resolutions, AGM/SGM minutes — plus a tracker for the annual returns due to the Registrar within two months of the AGM.

  2. Beneficiaries

    A verified Master Beneficiary Register with ID documents, households, succession, membership cards and receipts — so quorums and membership lists can be trusted.

  3. Administration

    Records management, correspondence and the delegation-of-authority matrix: nothing lost, everything found.

  4. Finance

    Budget, transactions, bank reconciliation and annual financial statements tracked from draft to filed.

  5. Procurement

    Requisitions, quotations, purchase orders, suppliers and payments — every rand authorised and traced.

  6. HR

    Staff records, payroll references, policies and the organogram.

  7. Land & Assets

    Parcels, allocations, leases, permits, infrastructure and maintenance — every hectare accounted for.

  8. Productivity

    Crops, orchards, timber, livestock, water, inputs, harvest and cost of production.

  9. Projects

    Pipeline, scorecards, business cases and delivery tracking with owners and evidence.

  10. Commercialisation

    Markets, partnerships, revenue streams and the investment-readiness evidence funders ask for.

  11. Performance

    The CPA360 Institutional Score, scored live from the CPA's own registers, plus monitoring & evaluation.

Outcomes

What a stronger institution looks like — for members, the Department and funders.

  • Stronger governance

    A functioning EXCO with minutes, resolutions and clear delegation, and an AGM held, minuted and filed on time.

  • Better records

    A verified beneficiary register and a complete Master File — so AGM quorums are reachable and membership lists stand up to scrutiny.

  • Financial accountability

    Budgets, reconciliations and audited statements, ready for the Registrar when they fall due.

  • Productive land

    Allocations documented, enterprises identified, production recorded — land that is used, not just held.

  • Managed projects

    Business cases, owners, deadlines and evidence on every project, visible to everyone who backs it.

  • Commercial readiness

    Market linkages, partnerships and diversified revenue in view, backed by records a partner can trust.

  • Investment readiness

    The institutional evidence a funder, a bank or a government partner needs to say yes — in one auditable place.
